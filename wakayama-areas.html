Prefecture Area Guide

Wakayama by Area

Wakayama Prefecture breaks down into a handful of distinct areas, each with its own character. Click a pin on the map, or scroll down, to explore each one.

Where each area sits within Wakayama
Wakayama Prefecture's real boundary (same map data as the main site) — click a pin to jump to that area.
Wakayama City & WakanouraCastle & Scenic Bay

The prefectural capital, home to a reconstructed castle and Wakanoura Bay, a coastal area celebrated in classical Japanese poetry.

  • Wakayama Castle, rebuilt and overlooking the city.
  • Wakanoura Bay, celebrated since ancient times in classical poetry.
  • A relaxed prefectural capital on the coast.
ShirahamaBeach & Ancient Onsen

A popular beach resort town known for its white sand beach and some of Japan's oldest hot springs.

  • Shirarahama Beach, known for its bright white sand.
  • Among the oldest documented hot springs in Japan.
  • Dramatic coastal rock formations nearby.
Kumano & NachikatsuuraJapan's Tallest Waterfall

Home to Nachi Falls, Japan's tallest waterfall, and a key stop on the sacred Kumano Kodo pilgrimage route.

  • Nachi Falls — Japan's tallest single-drop waterfall.
  • Seiganto-ji Temple's pagoda framed against the falls.
  • A major stop on the UNESCO-listed Kumano Kodo pilgrimage.
KoyasanSacred Mountain Monastery

A sacred mountaintop monastic complex and UNESCO World Heritage site, where visitors can stay overnight in centuries-old temple lodgings.

  • Okunoin — a vast, atmospheric cemetery among towering cedars.
  • Overnight stays (shukubo) at working temple lodgings.
  • A UNESCO World Heritage center of Shingon Buddhism.
