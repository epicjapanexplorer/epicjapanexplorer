Prefecture Area Guide

Miyagi by Area

Miyagi Prefecture breaks down into a handful of distinct areas, each with its own character. Click a pin on the map, or scroll down, to explore each one.

Where each area sits within Miyagi
Miyagi Prefecture's real boundary (same map data as the main site) — click a pin to jump to that area.
SendaiTohoku's Largest City

Tohoku's largest city, known as the "City of Trees" for its tree-lined boulevards, Date Masamune's castle ruins, and the lively Tanabata Festival each August.

  • Sendai Tanabata Festival — elaborate streamers fill the shopping arcades.
  • Aoba Castle ruins and a statue of feudal lord Date Masamune.
  • Zunda mochi — a local sweet made from mashed edamame.
MatsushimaOne of Japan's Three Great Views

One of Japan's "Three Great Views," a bay scattered with hundreds of pine-covered islets, best seen by sightseeing boat.

  • A scenic cruise around 260+ small pine-covered islands.
  • Zuiganji — a historic Zen temple founded in the 9th century.
  • Fresh grilled oysters, a Matsushima Bay specialty.
ZaoSnow Monsters & Crater Lake

A volcanic mountain range straddling Miyagi and Yamagata, famous for its winter "snow monsters" (frost-covered trees) and the turquoise crater lake Okama.

  • Winter "snow monsters" — trees encased in wind-sculpted frost.
  • Okama crater lake — a striking turquoise volcanic lake.
  • Zao Onsen, just across the border in Yamagata, for a soak after.
Ishinomaki & SanrikuFishing City & Coast

A rebuilt fishing city and gateway to Miyagi's stretch of the Sanriku coast, known for oysters, dramatic seascapes, and post-disaster recovery landmarks.

  • Fresh oysters and seafood from Ishinomaki's working port.
  • Kinkasan Island — a sacred island shrine off the coast.
  • Recovery memorial sites reflecting on the 2011 tsunami.
