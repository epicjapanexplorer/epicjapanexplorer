Prefecture Area Guide

Fukui by Area

Fukui Prefecture breaks down into a handful of distinct areas, each with its own character. Click a pin on the map, or scroll down, to explore each one.

Where each area sits within Fukui
Fukui Prefecture's real boundary (same map data as the main site) — click a pin to jump to that area.
EiheijiZen Monastery

One of the two head temples of Soto Zen Buddhism, a serene working monastery deep in a cedar forest, open to visitors and short stays.

  • A working Zen monastery, one of Soto Buddhism's two head temples.
  • Overnight stays with monks, including meditation and meals.
  • A dramatic wooden temple complex set among ancient cedars.
TojinboDramatic Sea Cliffs

A dramatic stretch of volcanic cliffs on the Sea of Japan, formed by rare columnar rock formations found in only a few places worldwide.

  • Rare columnar basalt cliffs, among only a few such sites worldwide.
  • Boat cruises beneath the dramatic cliff faces.
  • Striking sunset views over the Sea of Japan.
Awara OnsenFukui's Signature Onsen

Fukui's best-known hot spring town, popular for crab-season ryokan stays in winter.

  • A classic hot spring town popular for winter crab-season stays.
  • A relaxed base for exploring nearby Tojinbo.
  • Traditional ryokan hospitality.
TsurugaHistoric Port City

A historic port city on Wakasa Bay, known for its maritime heritage and as a gateway to the Hokuriku region.

  • A historic port with ties to early 20th-century international trade.
  • Kehi Jingu, one of the region's most important shrines.
  • A gateway city connecting to the wider Hokuriku region.
