Prefecture Area Guide

Ibaraki by Area

Ibaraki Prefecture breaks down into a handful of distinct areas, each with its own character. Click a pin on the map, or scroll down, to explore each one.

Where each area sits within Ibaraki
Ibaraki Prefecture's real boundary (same map data as the main site) — click a pin to jump to that area.
MitoPrefectural Capital

The prefectural capital, home to Kairaku-en — one of Japan's three great gardens, famous for its plum blossoms each spring.

  • Kairaku-en — one of Japan's three great gardens, best in plum season (Feb–Mar).
  • Mito Castle ruins and the adjacent Kodokan, once Japan's largest feudal school.
  • A compact, walkable city center.
OaraiCoastal Town

A relaxed coastal town known for its seaside shrine, major aquarium, and beaches — a popular day trip from Mito.

  • Oarai Isosaki Shrine — a torii gate standing right in the surf.
  • Aqua World Oarai — one of Japan's largest aquariums.
  • Sandy beaches popular for swimming and surfing in summer.
TsukubaScience City

A planned science city beneath Mt. Tsukuba, home to major research institutes, a space center, and panoramic mountain-top views.

  • JAXA Tsukuba Space Center — rockets and space exhibits.
  • Mt. Tsukuba ropeway — panoramic views over the Kanto plain.
  • A modern, walkable planned city with wide green boulevards.
KashimaAncient Shrine & Port

Home to Kashima Jingu, one of Japan's oldest and most revered shrines, and a major industrial port city with a storied football (soccer) tradition.

  • Kashima Jingu — one of Japan's oldest and most important shrines.
  • A sacred deer park within the shrine grounds.
  • Home of Kashima Antlers, one of Japan's most storied football clubs.
