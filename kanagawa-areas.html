Prefecture Area Guide

Kanagawa by Area

Kanagawa Prefecture breaks down into a handful of distinct areas, each with its own character. Click a pin on the map, or scroll down, to explore each one.

Where each area sits within Kanagawa
Kanagawa Prefecture's real boundary (same map data as the main site) — click a pin to jump to that area.
YokohamaJapan's Second City

Japan's second-largest city, known for its waterfront Minato Mirai skyline, historic Chinatown, and cosmopolitan port-city character.

  • Minato Mirai — a futuristic waterfront skyline and Ferris wheel.
  • Yokohama Chinatown, one of the largest in the world.
  • A historic port heritage dating to Japan's 19th-century opening.
KamakuraFormer Samurai Capital

A former samurai capital famous for its Great Buddha statue, dozens of temples and shrines, and a scenic coastal train line.

  • The Great Buddha (Daibutsu) — an 11m bronze statue dating to 1252.
  • Dozens of historic temples and shrines throughout the town.
  • The Enoden coastal train line, popular for its ocean views.
HakoneMountain Hot Springs & Mt. Fuji

A mountain hot spring resort area with views of Mt. Fuji, an active volcanic valley, and a scenic lake crossed by pirate-ship-style sightseeing boats.

  • Lake Ashi, with Mt. Fuji views and pirate-ship sightseeing cruises.
  • Owakudani volcanic valley, with steaming vents and black eggs.
  • Numerous hot spring ryokan across the mountains.
ShonanSurf Coast

A laid-back stretch of Pacific coastline, popular for surfing, beach towns, and views of Mt. Fuji across Sagami Bay.

  • A relaxed surf culture along Japan's most famous beach coastline.
  • Enoshima Island, a small scenic island connected by a bridge.
  • Views of Mt. Fuji across the water on clear days.
