Saga by Area

Saga Prefecture breaks down into a handful of distinct areas, each with its own character. Click a pin on the map, or scroll down, to explore each one.

Saga & YoshinogariAncient Archaeological Site

The prefectural capital, near Yoshinogari, one of Japan's largest and most important ancient archaeological sites.

  • Yoshinogari — one of Japan's largest Yayoi-period archaeological sites.
  • Reconstructed ancient moated settlement structures.
  • A rare window into Japan's ancient Yayoi culture.
KaratsuCastle & Kunchi Festival

A historic castle and pottery town, home to the elaborate Karatsu Kunchi festival floats each autumn.

  • Karatsu Kunchi — an elaborate autumn festival with giant floats.
  • Karatsu-yaki, a distinguished local pottery tradition.
  • A scenic castle overlooking the bay.
Arita & ImariBirthplace of Japanese Porcelain

The birthplace of Japanese porcelain, with centuries of continuous ceramic-making tradition and working kiln towns.

  • The birthplace of Japanese porcelain production.
  • Centuries-old working kilns and porcelain shops.
  • The annual Arita Ceramics Fair, a major pottery event.
Ureshino & TakeoSmooth-Water Hot Springs

A pair of historic hot spring towns known for smooth, skin-softening water and traditional ryokan hospitality.

  • Hot spring water noted for its smooth, skin-softening quality.
  • Traditional ryokan hospitality in a quieter setting.
  • A relaxed alternative to more crowded onsen towns.
