Prefecture Area Guide

Hokkaido by Area

Hokkaido Prefecture breaks down into a handful of distinct areas, each with its own character. Click a pin on the map, or scroll down, to explore each one.

Sapporo & OtaruCapital & Canal Town

Hokkaido's lively capital, known for its grid-planned streets, beer heritage, and winter Snow Festival, paired with the nearby canal town of Otaru — famous for its gas-lit warehouse district and glassware shops.

  • Sapporo Snow Festival (February) — massive snow and ice sculptures fill Odori Park.
  • Otaru Canal — a gas-lit, glass-crafting harbor town just 30 minutes from Sapporo.
  • Sapporo Beer Museum and the lively Susukino dining district.
HakodatePort City & Night Views

A historic port city with a dramatic night view from Mt. Hakodate, a Western-influenced harbor district, and one of Japan's best morning seafood markets.

  • Mt. Hakodate ropeway — one of Japan's three great night views.
  • Motomachi district — Meiji-era Western-style buildings and churches.
  • Hakodate Morning Market — fresh uni, crab, and seafood bowls at dawn.
Furano & BieiLavender Fields

Rolling patchwork hills and vivid lavender fields in summer, framed by the Tokachi mountains — one of Hokkaido's most photographed rural landscapes.

  • Farm Tomita — Furano's iconic lavender fields, best in July.
  • Biei's "Patchwork Road" — undulating farmland in every shade of green.
  • A popular winter ski resort as well, with powder snow slopes.
Asahikawa & DaisetsuzanZoo & Mountain Gateway

Home to Asahiyama Zoo's innovative enclosures and gateway to Daisetsuzan National Park, Hokkaido's largest and wildest mountain range.

  • Asahiyama Zoo — famous for its immersive penguin and polar bear enclosures.
  • Daisetsuzan National Park — Hokkaido's "roof," with Japan's earliest autumn colors.
  • Sounkyo Onsen — a hot spring town at the entrance to a dramatic gorge.
Shiretoko & E. HokkaidoWilderness Peninsula

A remote, UNESCO-listed peninsula of pristine wilderness, drift ice, and brown bears, alongside the wide-open lakes and pastures of Hokkaido's eastern frontier.

  • Shiretoko National Park — a UNESCO World Heritage site rich in wildlife.
  • Drift ice viewing near Abashiri (January–March).
  • Lake Mashu and Lake Akan — some of Japan's clearest caldera lakes.
