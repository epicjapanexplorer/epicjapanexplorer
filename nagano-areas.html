Prefecture Area Guide

Nagano by Area

Nagano Prefecture breaks down into a handful of distinct areas, each with its own character. Click a pin on the map, or scroll down, to explore each one.

Where each area sits within Nagano
Nagano Prefecture's real boundary (same map data as the main site) — click a pin to jump to that area.
Nagano CityTemple Capital

The prefectural capital, centered on Zenkoji, one of Japan's most important and historic Buddhist temples.

  • Zenkoji — one of Japan's oldest and most revered temples.
  • A temple town atmosphere along the approach street.
  • Host city of the 1998 Winter Olympics.
MatsumotoOriginal Castle Town

Home to one of Japan's most beautiful original castles, its black-walled keep reflected in a surrounding moat.

  • Matsumoto Castle — one of Japan's few original (non-rebuilt) castles.
  • A striking black exterior, earning it the nickname "Crow Castle."
  • A walkable historic town center with craft shops and cafes.
KamikochiPristine Alpine Valley

A pristine, car-free highland valley in the Northern Alps, considered one of Japan's most spectacular mountain landscapes.

  • A car-free valley, accessible only by shuttle bus or taxi.
  • Dramatic Northern Alps peaks reflected in the Azusa River.
  • Extensive hiking trails for all levels.
KaruizawaHighland Resort Town

A refined highland resort town long favored as a cool summer retreat, with outlet shopping and forested cycling paths.

  • A cool-climate retreat long favored by Tokyo's elite.
  • Karuizawa Prince Shopping Plaza, a major outlet mall.
  • Forested cycling paths and a walkable main street.
HakubaPremier Ski Resort

A premier ski and mountain resort village in the Northern Alps, popular with international visitors for its powder snow and alpine scenery.

  • Some of Japan's best powder snow skiing and snowboarding.
  • A former Winter Olympics venue (1998).
  • Summer hiking and mountain scenery in the off-season.
