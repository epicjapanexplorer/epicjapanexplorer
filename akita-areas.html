Akita by Area

Akita Prefecture breaks down into a handful of distinct areas, each with its own character. Click a pin on the map, or scroll down, to explore each one.

Akita CityPrefectural Capital

The prefectural capital, home to a striking Tadao Ando-designed art museum and the summer Kanto Festival, where illuminated bamboo poles are balanced by hand.

  • Akita Museum of Art — architecture by Tadao Ando.
  • Kanto Festival (early August) — balancing poles hung with lit lanterns.
  • Akita Folk Performing Arts Center — try the Kanto pole yourself.
OgaPeninsula & Folklore

A rugged peninsula on the Sea of Japan, famous as the home of the fearsome-looking Namahage folk deities who visit homes each New Year's Eve.

  • Namahage Museum — an extensive mask collection and live demonstrations.
  • Mt. Kanpu Rotating Observation Deck — panoramic peninsula views.
  • Dramatic sea cliffs along the peninsula's coastal drive.
KakunodateSamurai Town

A beautifully preserved Edo-period samurai town often called the "Little Kyoto of Michinoku," known for its weeping cherry trees along the old lanes.

  • Preserved samurai residences, some open to the public.
  • Hinokinai River Cherry Blossom Avenue in spring.
  • Best visited late April to early May for peak blossoms.
Lake TazawaJapan's Deepest Lake

Japan's deepest lake, striking for its cobalt-blue water that never fully freezes, with the rustic Nyuto Onsen hot springs tucked in the mountains nearby.

  • Tatsuko Statue — a golden statue tied to local legend.
  • Lake cruises around the shoreline.
  • Nyuto Onsen — remote, rustic hot spring inns nearby.
