Prefecture Area Guide

Yamagata by Area

Yamagata Prefecture breaks down into a handful of distinct areas, each with its own character. Click a pin on the map, or scroll down, to explore each one.

Where each area sits within Yamagata
Yamagata Prefecture's real boundary (same map data as the main site) — click a pin to jump to that area.
Yamagata City & ZaoBasin City & Zao

A castle-ruin city in a mountain basin, gateway to the Zao hot springs and the region's famous winter "snow monster" frosted trees.

  • Yamagata Castle ruins and surrounding park.
  • Zao Onsen — hot springs and winter snow monsters nearby.
  • Basin climate with hot summers and heavy winter snow.
YonezawaWagyu & Samurai Heritage

Home to one of Japan's most prized wagyu beef brands, with a well-preserved samurai heritage tied to the Uesugi clan.

  • Yonezawa beef — among Japan's most celebrated wagyu.
  • Uesugi Shrine, dedicated to the influential Uesugi clan.
  • A compact, walkable historic town center.
Ginzan OnsenGaslamp Onsen Village

A tiny, magical hot spring town of wooden ryokan lit by gaslamps along a snowy riverside street — one of Japan's most photogenic onsen villages.

  • Gaslit streets at dusk — especially magical in winter snow.
  • Multi-story wooden ryokan lining a narrow river.
  • Car-free — reached by a short walk or bus from the parking area.
Sakata & TsuruokaSea of Japan Port Towns

Historic Sea of Japan port towns, home to the Shonai rice-growing plain, the sacred Three Mountains of Dewa pilgrimage route, and refined regional cuisine.

  • Sanno Kurashiki — a preserved Edo-period rice warehouse district.
  • The Three Mountains of Dewa — a centuries-old pilgrimage route.
  • Fresh Shonai-plain rice and Sea of Japan seafood.
