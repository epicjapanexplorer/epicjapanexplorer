Iwate by Area

Iwate Prefecture breaks down into a handful of distinct areas, each with its own character. Click a pin on the map, or scroll down, to explore each one.

Where each area sits within Iwate

MoriokaRiverside Capital

A compact, riverside capital where three rivers meet, known for its castle ruins, "wanko soba" all-you-can-eat noodle tradition, and easy access to nearby mountains.

  • Wanko soba — bite-sized soba served endlessly, bowl after bowl.
  • Morioka Castle Ruins Park — cherry blossoms over stone ramparts.
  • Gozaku district — preserved Meiji and Taisho-era storefronts.
HiraizumiUNESCO World Heritage

A UNESCO World Heritage former capital of the northern Fujiwara clan, centered on the gold-leafed Konjikido hall at Chuson-ji temple and the Pure Land garden at Motsu-ji.

  • Chuson-ji's Konjikido — an entire hall covered in gold leaf.
  • Motsu-ji — a Heian-era Pure Land paradise garden.
  • A UNESCO World Heritage Site reflecting 12th-century Buddhist ideals.
SanrikuRias Coastline

A dramatic rias coastline of steep cliffs and fishing villages, including the Geibikei Gorge boat ride, rebuilt after the 2011 tsunami into a resilient, scenic coastal region.

  • Geibikei Gorge — a tranquil boat ride beneath towering rock walls.
  • Jodogahama Beach — white rock formations against blue water.
  • Fishing towns rebuilt with a strong spirit of recovery since 2011.
