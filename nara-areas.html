Prefecture Area Guide

Nara by Area

Nara Prefecture breaks down into a handful of distinct areas, each with its own character. Click a pin on the map, or scroll down, to explore each one.

Where each area sits within Nara
Nara Prefecture's real boundary (same map data as the main site) — click a pin to jump to that area.
Nara CityDeer Park & Great Buddha

Japan's first permanent capital, home to Nara Park's free-roaming deer, the Great Buddha of Todai-ji, and Kasuga Taisha shrine.

  • Nara Park's hundreds of free-roaming, bowing deer.
  • Todai-ji's Great Buddha, housed in a massive wooden hall.
  • Kasuga Taisha, famous for its rows of stone and bronze lanterns.
Horyuji & IkarugaWorld's Oldest Wooden Buildings

Home to Horyuji, believed to include the oldest wooden buildings in the world, and a quiet area rich in early Buddhist heritage.

  • Horyuji — believed to house the world's oldest wooden structures.
  • A UNESCO World Heritage Site tied to early Japanese Buddhism.
  • A quiet, less-crowded alternative to central Nara.
YoshinoLegendary Cherry Blossoms

One of Japan's most celebrated cherry blossom destinations, with thousands of trees cascading down a mountainside.

  • Thousands of cherry trees blooming across the mountainside.
  • Considered one of Japan's top three cherry blossom spots.
  • Also a historic site tied to medieval Japanese warrior legends.
Asuka & KashiharaCradle of Japanese Civilization

The cradle of early Japanese civilization, dotted with ancient burial mounds, ruins, and Japan's first capital sites.

  • Ancient stone burial mounds and mysterious carved monuments.
  • Japan's earliest known capital sites.
  • A rural, unhurried area rich in ancient history.
